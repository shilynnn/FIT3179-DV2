Shell Shock: How Australia Became a Nut Superpower

In less than two decades, almonds and macadamias have turned the Murray River and the Queensland coast into one of the world's great nut bowls. Where are they grown, who buys them, and can the boom survive drought, floods and global competition?

Chapter 1

Planted in the 2000s, harvested today

The almond crop has more than quadrupled since 2009

Top: almond production, kernel tonnes. Bottom: hectares of new orchards planted each year. Hover the line for exact values.

An almond tree takes roughly five to seven years to reach full production. The planting waves of 2006–07 and 2016–17 (bottom bars) are the reason the crop jumped in 2013 and again after 2019.

Production is not a straight line, though. A poor 2023 season cut the crop by a fifth before it bounced back to a record 163,148 tonnes in 2025.

Almonds earn seven of every ten nut dollars

Australia commercially grows seven tree nuts, but the industry is lopsided. Almonds (71%) and macadamias (16%) together make up close to nine-tenths of the $1.23 billion farm-gate value.

New almond orchards are moving east

Hectares of almond orchards, 2023 vs 2026 mapping. Labels show the change.

Almonds need hot, dry summers and reliable irrigation, so nearly all orchards sit along the Murray River system, in Sunraysia, the Riverina and South Australia's Riverland.

Since 2023 the national orchard area has grown by around 9,500 hectares. Most of that growth is in the Riverina and Riverland, while Victorian Sunraysia, the traditional heartland, has shrunk slightly.

Macadamias, native to the rainforests of south-east Queensland and northern NSW, need warm, humid weather. Bundaberg alone harvested almost two-thirds of the 2025 crop.

At the mercy of the weather

Yet the industry expects the crop to double by 2030

Solid: actual crop. Dashed: Australian Macadamia Society forecast.

Heavy, prolonged rain damaged crops and kept growers out of their orchards in 2025. Many growers in NSW and Queensland reported their lowest crops in more than a decade. Bundaberg's young orchards were the exception, producing a record harvest.

China leads, India holds second, Türkiye climbs

Rank of the top five almond export destinations by volume. Hover a line to highlight a country.

About four in every five tonnes of Australian almonds are exported. China has been the top buyer every year, but its purchases swing sharply, from about 51,000 tonnes in 2021/22 to 34,000 tonnes in 2022/23.

India has held second place every year, while Germany dropped out of the top five and Türkiye entered it in 2021/22. Spreading sales across more markets lowers the risk of relying on one buyer.

Chapter 5

Exporting almonds, importing hazelnuts

We sell what we grow well and buy what we don't

2023/24. Left: imports as a share of domestic supply. Right: exports as a share of production (macadamia figure: 2025 crop, Australian Macadamia Society).

Australia is a net exporter of almonds and macadamias. For smaller crops the picture flips: 86% of the hazelnuts Australians eat are imported, mostly from Türkiye, and over a third of our walnuts and pistachios come from overseas, mainly the United States.

What next?

The macadamia is Australia's native nut, but other countries have planted it on a huge scale. By 2030, China and South Africa could each grow more than Australia's entire forecast crop.

To stay competitive, Australia's nut industry is betting on quality, new markets such as India and the Middle East, and self-fertile almond varieties that rely less on hired beehives for pollination.
